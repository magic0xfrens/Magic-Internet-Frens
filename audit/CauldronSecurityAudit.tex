\section{Abstract}

The Cauldron is a self-perpetuating token launchpad built atop Uniswap~v4. A
single genesis event seeds an ERC-20 (\emph{iteration~1}) paired with native ETH
in a v4 pool governed by a custom hook. The hook meters 24-hour trading volume
entirely on-chain; when volume falls below a death threshold the token is
declared \emph{dead}, its liquidity is recovered, and a permissionless
\texttt{relaunch()} mints the next iteration --- chosen by NFT-holder governance
--- reusing the recovered ETH as fresh liquidity. Holders of a dead iteration
migrate their balance $1{:}1$ into the live one; a founding NFT guild earns a
perpetual fee dividend.

We audited the contracts that constitute this machine
(\textasciitilde3{,}900 lines of Solidity) for access-control, logic,
reentrancy, arithmetic, oracle, and economic vulnerabilities, as well as gas and
architectural quality. Across two engagements we report \textbf{two High},
\textbf{two Medium}, and a set of \textbf{Low / Informational} findings. The most
material issues --- a privilege that let the hook owner drain the self-funding ETH
reserve (F-01); a token-freeze interaction that stranded the genesis airdrop
(F-02); and, in revision~2, a fee-exemption that any trader could invoke by
forging \texttt{hookData} to bypass the entire fee engine (F-13) --- were all
\textbf{remediated and regression-tested} during the engagement. Revision~2 also
introduced a \emph{pluggable-module} architecture (a death-checker plus surtax /
odds / mint-curve policies) that lets the protocol's economic \emph{rules} be
upgraded without an upgradeable proxy; we verify it is bounded and fund-safe by
construction (clamped, \texttt{try/catch} fallback, view-only, never in a
fund-moving path). Remaining items are centralization trade-offs mitigated by an
immutable on-chain timelock and documented mainnet operational requirements.

\section{Methodology}

The review followed a five-phase process:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Preparation.} Pragma and dependency inventory; inheritance and
        privilege mapping; identification of all external / \texttt{payable}
        entrypoints and value-bearing flows.
  \item \textbf{Static analysis.} Manual control-flow tracing of every
        state-changing path; checks-effects-interactions (CEI) verification;
        arithmetic and \texttt{unchecked} review.
  \item \textbf{Vulnerability assessment.} Evaluation against the OWASP Smart
        Contract Top~10 (2025): access control, logic errors, reentrancy, flash
        loans, input validation, oracle manipulation, unchecked calls.
  \item \textbf{Optimization \& architecture.} Storage layout, bytecode budget
        (EIP-170), dead code, and modularity.
  \item \textbf{Remediation \& verification.} Fixes authored, compiled under the
        project's \texttt{via\_ir} profile, and covered by new Foundry tests.
\end{enumerate}

Severity is the product of \emph{impact} (fund loss, protocol halt, degraded
guarantees) and \emph{likelihood} (preconditions, attacker capability):
\sev{sevCrit}{Critical}, \sev{sevHigh}{High}, \sev{sevMed}{Medium},
\sev{sevLow}{Low}, \sev{sevInfo}{Informational}.

The codebase was reviewed in \textbf{two independent passes}. The first
established the findings and remediations; a second, blank-slate pass
re-derived every cross-contract value flow from scratch (the basis for
Figure~\ref{fig:arch}) to confirm the model and catch anything the first pass
normalised away --- it surfaced finding F-11.

\paragraph{Lifecycle in eight movements.}
\textbf{(1) Genesis:} traders mint OG MiFrens; ETH pools in the presale until
sellout, then \texttt{finalize()} forwards it to \texttt{summon()}, which mints
iteration~1, reserves the guild airdrop, and seeds the v4 pool.
\textbf{(2) Trade:} every swap pays an ETH fee the hook splits into
\emph{dividend} (1\% + launch surtax), \emph{floor vault}, and \emph{relaunch
reserve}. \textbf{(3) Earn NFTs:} swap volume accrues gacha credit; the router
commits and resolves lottery tickets that mint the collection.
\textbf{(4) Floor:} NFT holders may \texttt{redeem} to burn an NFT for its equal
slice of the vault. \textbf{(5) Dividend:} genesis holders \texttt{claim} a
perpetual share of every iteration's fees. \textbf{(6) Airdrop:} genesis holders
\texttt{claimGenesis} their reserved iteration-1 tokens (per-tokenId).
\textbf{(7) Death \& rebirth:} when 24h volume dies, \texttt{relaunch()} recovers
ETH from the LP + vault + reserve and seeds the governance-chosen next iteration,
paying the caller a 1\% bounty. \textbf{(8) Carry forward:} holders of a dead
iteration \texttt{claimByBurn} their balance $1{:}1$ into the live token.

\section{Detailed Findings}

% ---- F1 ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-01 --- Hook owner can drain the relaunch ETH reserve \sev{sevHigh}{(High)}}

\begin{finding}{sevHigh}{F-01 · CauldronHook · Access Control · Fund Loss}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{CauldronHook.setRegistry}, \texttt{releaseRelaunchETH}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} The hook accumulates ETH swap fees in \texttt{relaunchETH},
the self-funding reserve that seeds each new iteration. Only the address stored
in \texttt{registry} may withdraw it:

\begin{lstlisting}
function releaseRelaunchETH() external returns (uint256 amount) {
    if (msg.sender != registry) revert OnlyRegistry();
    amount = relaunchETH; relaunchETH = 0;
    (bool ok, ) = registry.call{value: amount}(""); require(ok, "ETH transfer failed");
}
\end{lstlisting}

However, \texttt{registry} was set by a \emph{mutable} owner-only setter, and the
hook's ownership is never transferred away from the deploying EOA:

\begin{lstlisting}
function setRegistry(address _registry) external onlyOwner { registry = _registry; }  // BEFORE
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Impact.} The hook owner could call
\texttt{setRegistry(attacker)} and then, from \texttt{attacker}, call
\texttt{releaseRelaunchETH()} --- transferring the entire accrued ETH reserve to
themselves. Because the reserve is precisely the capital that funds every future
relaunch, this both steals funds \emph{and} can brick the protocol's
self-perpetuation. The registry itself had already been hardened with an
immutable emergency timelock, but this parallel drain path on the hook had no
such control, making it the single most direct fund-loss vector in the system.

\textbf{Recommendation \& remediation.} Make \texttt{registry} write-once. There
is exactly one registry per deployment, so a one-time setter fully preserves
intended behaviour while removing the drain path:

\begin{lstlisting}
error RegistryAlreadySet();
function setRegistry(address _registry) external onlyOwner {              // AFTER (fixed)
    if (registry != address(0)) revert RegistryAlreadySet();
    if (_registry == address(0)) revert ZeroAddress();
    registry = _registry;
}
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Status: Fixed.} Regression suite \texttt{CauldronHookAccess.t.sol}
asserts the setter is one-time, rejects the zero address, and is owner-gated.

% ---- F2 ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-02 --- Genesis airdrop stranded by death-freeze after relaunch \sev{sevMed}{(Medium)}}

\begin{finding}{sevMed}{F-02 · CauldronToken / CauldronRegistry · Logic}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{CauldronToken.\_update}, \texttt{CauldronRegistry.claimGenesis}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} Each iteration's ERC-20 \emph{freezes} on death: once
\texttt{markDead()} is called, user transfers revert with \texttt{TokenIsDead()}.
The freeze deliberately exempts mints, burns, and the PoolManager (for LP
recovery), but \emph{not} the registry:

\begin{lstlisting}
if (from != address(0) && to != address(0) && from != poolManager && !isAlive) {
    revert TokenIsDead();   // BEFORE: registry not exempt
}
\end{lstlisting}

The founding-guild genesis airdrop, however, pays out by \emph{transferring} the
reserved iteration-1 tokens from the registry:

\begin{lstlisting}
IERC20(generationToken[1]).transfer(msg.sender, amount);   // claimGenesis
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Impact.} After the first \texttt{relaunch()} froze iteration~1, every
subsequent \texttt{claimGenesis()} reverted with \texttt{TokenIsDead}
(\texttt{0xfe641f95}). The entire unclaimed airdrop reserve
(\textasciitilde$77.7$M tokens at $69{,}937$ per NFT) became permanently
unclaimable --- a griefing / value-loss condition triggered by ordinary protocol
operation, with no attacker required.

\textbf{Recommendation \& remediation.} Exempt the registry from the freeze,
mirroring the existing PoolManager exemption. The registry only ever moves tokens
for defined distributions, so this preserves the anti-trading intent:

\begin{lstlisting}
if (from != address(0) && to != address(0)
    && from != poolManager && from != registry && !isAlive) {   // AFTER (fixed)
    revert TokenIsDead();
}
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Status: Fixed.} \texttt{CauldronTokenFreeze.t.sol} proves the registry
can pay out after death while user-to-user transfers remain frozen and
burn / PoolManager paths are unaffected.

% ---- F3 ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-03 --- Privileged-role centralization \sev{sevMed}{(Medium)}}

\begin{finding}{sevMed}{F-03 · CauldronRegistry / CauldronHook · Centralization}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{emergencyWithdrawLP}, \texttt{emergencySweep};
hook \texttt{setVault}, \texttt{setGuild}, \texttt{setTreasury}, \texttt{setCollection}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} Two privileged roles retain material power after genesis:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item The registry \texttt{emergencyAdmin} can withdraw any generation's LP
        (ETH + tokens) and sweep the registry's ETH / ERC-20 balances --- including
        the unclaimed genesis reserve.
  \item The hook \texttt{owner} can repoint the fee sinks
        (\texttt{setVault}, \texttt{setGuild}, \texttt{setCollection},
        \texttt{setTreasury}) and thereby redirect \emph{future} fee flows.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Impact.} A compromised or malicious admin could seize protocol-owned
liquidity or divert future fees. This is the classic launchpad ``deployer rug''
surface.

\textbf{Mitigations in place.} The registry's emergency actions are governed by
an \emph{immutable} timelock (\texttt{emergencyDelay}, set at construction and
un-lowerable) with an on-chain \texttt{armEmergency()} announcement, so on
mainnet a withdrawal must be armed and a fixed delay elapsed --- holders observe
the \texttt{EmergencyArmed} event and can exit first. Owner-configuration of the
registry is \emph{frozen} post-deploy because ownership is transferred to the
autonomous presale contract, which exposes no such setters. The F-01 fix removes
the one owner path that led to direct theft (reserve drain).

\textbf{Recommendation.} For mainnet: (i) set \texttt{emergencyAdmin} to a Gnosis
Safe multisig and \texttt{emergencyDelay} to $\geq 7$ days; (ii) transfer the
hook's ownership to the same multisig (ideally behind a timelock); (iii) publish
a decommissioning path that renounces the hook owner and emergency admin once the
machine is battle-tested. \textbf{Status: Mitigated} (timelock live; multisig is
a deployment-time operational requirement).

% ---- F4 ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-04 --- Weak randomness in gacha resolution fallback \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}

\begin{finding}{sevLow}{F-04 · CauldronHook · Randomness}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{resolveTickets}, \texttt{CauldronCollection.\_rollRarity}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} The crystal gacha is a commit-reveal lottery: a ticket's
outcome is seeded by \texttt{blockhash(commitBlock)}, unknown at commit time.
\texttt{blockhash} is only available for the most recent 256 blocks; beyond that
the code falls back to a \emph{deterministic} seed derivable at commit time:

\begin{lstlisting}
bytes32 bh = blockhash(b.commitBlock);
if (bh == 0) bh = keccak256(abi.encodePacked("CAULDRON_TICKET_FALLBACK", b.commitBlock, bi));
\end{lstlisting}

If resolution is delayed beyond 256 blocks, a sophisticated player who can
predict the batch index can foresee the outcome. NFT rarity is likewise rolled
from \texttt{blockhash}/\texttt{block.prevrandao}, which a block proposer can
marginally bias.

\textbf{Impact.} Low. Outcomes are grindable only under a $>$256-block resolution
lag (resolution is permissionless and normally immediate), win \emph{odds} and
\emph{pity} are fixed at commit, and the value at stake is a single volume-minted
NFT whose floor is already funded by the swap fee. No pooled funds are at risk.

\textbf{Recommendation.} Treat a stale ($>$256-block) commit as a forced miss, or
require resolution within the blockhash window; for high-value randomness use a
VRF. \textbf{Status: Acknowledged} (accepted given value at stake).

% ---- F5 ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-05 --- Per-iteration collections cannot enable royalty enforcement \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}

\begin{finding}{sevLow}{F-05 · CauldronFactory / CauldronCollection · Design}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{CauldronCollection.setTransferValidator / setRarityOdds}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} A factory-deployed collection records its
\texttt{deployer} as \texttt{msg.sender} --- i.e. the \texttt{CauldronFactory}.
The ERC-721C \texttt{setTransferValidator} and \texttt{setRarityOdds} setters are
gated to that deployer, but the factory exposes no method to call them after
construction. They are therefore permanently uncallable for per-iteration
(gen~1, gen~3+) collections.

\textbf{Impact.} Low. EIP-2981 royalties are still \emph{declared} (and honoured
by compliant marketplaces), but on-chain enforcement (blocking non-paying
markets) can never be switched on for these collections, and rarity odds are
fixed at the sensible default. The canonical MiFrens collection (gen~1/2) is
unaffected --- its deployer is the presale EOA, which can set a validator.

\textbf{Recommendation.} Have the factory forward a one-time
\texttt{setTransferValidator} during \texttt{deployBrew}, or pass the registry as
the collection \texttt{deployer}. \textbf{Status: Acknowledged.}

\subsection{F-06 --- Unchecked ERC-20 return values \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}

\begin{finding}{sevLow}{F-06 · Multiple · Unchecked Calls}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{registry.claimGenesis / emergencySweep};
\texttt{hook.withdrawTokenFees}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} Several \texttt{IERC20.transfer} calls ignore the boolean
return value. Non-standard tokens that return \texttt{false} instead of reverting
would fail silently.

\textbf{Impact.} Low. In normal operation the only token moved is the in-house
\texttt{CauldronToken}, an OpenZeppelin ERC-20 that reverts on failure. The
arbitrary-token path (\texttt{emergencySweep}) is admin-only and best-effort.

\textbf{Recommendation.} Adopt OpenZeppelin \texttt{SafeERC20}
(\texttt{safeTransfer}). \textbf{Status: Acknowledged} (no in-house impact; the
gacha router already uses safe-transfer wrappers).

% ---- F11 -----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-11 --- Genesis tranche is floor-redeemable on iteration~2 \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}

\begin{finding}{sevLow}{F-11 · CauldronVault / MiFrensDividend · Economic / Logic}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{CauldronVault.redeem}; \texttt{MiFrensDividend} share
accounting. Surfaced during the independent second-pass review.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} Iteration~2 does not deploy a fresh collection --- it
\emph{continues} the canonical MiFrens collection, wiring a new floor vault whose
solvency basis is the whole collection:
\[
  \texttt{floorPerNFT} = \frac{\texttt{balance}}{\texttt{totalMinted} - \texttt{redeemed}}.
\]
\texttt{redeem(tokenId)} accepts \emph{any} token the caller owns and burns it via
\texttt{burnFromVault}, with no lower bound on the id. The genesis tranche
(ids $1\ldots\texttt{GENESIS\_SUPPLY}$) is therefore floor-redeemable during
iteration~2.

\textbf{Impact.} A founding-guild member can redeem an OG genesis MiFren for a
floor slice, which \emph{burns} it. Because \texttt{MiFrensDividend.SHARES} is
fixed at the genesis supply while the burned id's \texttt{ownerOf} now reverts,
that share becomes permanently unclaimable: the accumulator keeps allocating a
per-share amount to a token nobody can claim, \textbf{stranding that ETH forever}
(a slow dividend leak proportional to burned genesis ids). The holder also
forfeits the token's perpetual governance vote. No third party is harmed and no
pooled funds are stolen --- the loss is self-inflicted plus a protocol-level dust
leak --- but it violates the ``genesis is a permanent share'' invariant.

\textbf{Recommendation.} Exclude genesis ids from redemption on the continued
collection (e.g. the vault rejects \texttt{tokenId $\leq$ GENESIS\_SUPPLY}), so
only volume-minted NFTs are floor-backed; or have the dividend track a live share
count rather than a fixed \texttt{SHARES}. \textbf{Status: Acknowledged}
(iteration-2-only; no impact on the currently live iteration~1).

% ---- F7--F10 -------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-07 to F-10 --- Informational}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{F-07 Dead code (vestigial fee accounting).} The hook fees are
        taken exclusively in ETH; \texttt{accumulatedTokenFees} is never written,
        so \texttt{withdrawTokenFees} / \texttt{treasury} are unreachable state.
        Recommend removal to shrink the attack surface and bytecode.
  \item \textbf{F-08 Late tickets mint into a closed vault.} Gacha tickets
        committed in the same block as a relaunch resolve afterwards and mint into
        the now-closed collection, whose floor vault has swept. Such NFTs retain
        art but no redeemable floor. Documented and accepted; \texttt{relaunch()}
        already drains matured tickets \emph{before} closing the vault.
  \item \textbf{F-09 Redundant assignments in \texttt{relaunch()}.} Genesis
        metadata defaults are assigned then immediately overwritten by the
        governor's winning spec (guaranteed to exist by the \texttt{NoProposal}
        guard). Harmless; minor gas / readability.
  \item \textbf{F-10 Balance-delta accounting in \texttt{\_removeLiquidity}.}
        Recovered amounts are computed as post-minus-pre balance deltas. Correct
        under current atomic flows; would misreport if an unexpected inflow
        occurred mid-call. Prefer explicit return values from the settlement.
\end{itemize}

\section{Revision~2 Findings — Modules, Snipe \& Token Rewrite}

% ---- F-13 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-13 --- Fee exemption bypassable via attacker-controlled hookData \sev{sevHigh}{(High)}}

\begin{finding}{sevHigh}{F-13 · CauldronHook · Access Control / Logic · Fee Evasion}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{\_isExemptPlayer}, \texttt{\_beforeSwap}, \texttt{\_afterSwap}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} To fund the OG airdrop at launch without diluting presalers,
the protocol flags one wallet (the deployer / \texttt{LaunchSniper}) as
\emph{fee-exempt} so its one-time buy skips the base tax and anti-sniper surtax.
The exemption was read from the swap's \texttt{hookData}:

\begin{lstlisting}
function _isExemptPlayer(bytes calldata hookData) private view returns (bool) {   // BEFORE
    if (hookData.length < 32) return false;
    return taxExempt[abi.decode(hookData, (address))];
}
\end{lstlisting}

In v4, \texttt{hookData} is supplied by the swap caller. The gacha router honestly
tags it with the real player, but a trader swapping \emph{directly} through the
PoolManager controls \texttt{hookData} entirely.

\textbf{Impact.} \emph{Any} trader could set
\texttt{hookData = abi.encode(exemptAddress)} and pay \textbf{zero hook fee} on
every swap --- collapsing the protocol's entire economic engine, since that fee is
what funds the floor vault, the genesis dividend, and the self-funding relaunch
reserve. No theft of principal, but a total, permanent bypass of fee capture: a
protocol-wide value-leak exploitable by anyone, permissionlessly.

\textbf{Remediation.} Honour the exemption only for swaps arriving from a
\emph{trusted opener} (the gacha router), whose tag can be believed. A direct
swapper's \texttt{sender} is not an opener, so its \texttt{hookData} is ignored:

\begin{lstlisting}
function _isExemptPlayer(address sender, bytes calldata hookData) private view returns (bool) {
    if (!isOpener[sender]) return false;           // AFTER: only trust the router's tag
    if (hookData.length < 32) return false;
    return taxExempt[abi.decode(hookData, (address))];
}
\end{lstlisting}

The intended launch buy (deployer $\rightarrow$ router $\rightarrow$ pool) still
works because the router is a registered opener. \textbf{Status: Fixed}, with a
regression test asserting the opener gate.

% ---- F-12 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-12 --- Genesis bonus claimable by non-genesis tokenIds \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}

\begin{finding}{sevLow}{F-12 · CauldronRegistry · Input Validation}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{claimGenesis}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} \texttt{claimGenesis(tokenId)} pays a founder's token bonus
per MiFren. The reserve is sized for the genesis tranche
(\texttt{1..genesisShares}), but the function did not bound \texttt{tokenId} to
that range. Once iteration~2 mints volume MiFrens (ids $>$ \texttt{genesisShares}),

\textbf{Remediation.} \texttt{if (mifrenTokenId == 0 || mifrenTokenId >

% ---- F-14 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-14 --- Pluggable policy modules: gas-grief \& owner-set surface \sev{sevInfo}{(Info)}}

\begin{finding}{sevInfo}{F-14 · CauldronHook · Upgradeability / DoS}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{deathChecker}, \texttt{surtaxPolicy}, \texttt{oddsPolicy},
\texttt{curvePolicy} and their delegating call-sites.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} To allow the death rule and the surtax / odds / mint curves
to be \emph{upgraded without a proxy}, the hook delegates to optional external
modules. This adds two surfaces: (i) a module is an external call in the swap hot
path, so a pathological module could grief gas; (ii) the modules are owner/registry
settable.

\textbf{Design mitigations (verified sound).} The pattern is deliberately
defensive: every delegation is (a) wrapped in \texttt{try/catch} so a reverting
module \emph{always} falls back to the built-in rule and can never brick a swap or
relaunch; (b) \emph{clamped} to the same hard caps as the built-ins
(\texttt{MAX\_SNIPE\_BPS}, \texttt{ODDS\_HARD\_CAP\_BPS}, non-zero curve cost), so a
module cannot exceed the surtax ceiling, guarantee a gacha win, or set a
zero-cost mint; (c) a pure \texttt{view} (staticcall), so it cannot reenter or
mutate state; and (d) never in a fund-moving path --- modules return \emph{numbers}
only. The fund flows (fixed supply, 1:1 migration, LP recycling) remain immutable;
only policy math is swappable.

\textbf{Residual.} The setters share the F-03 owner-key surface. Recommendation:
route \texttt{setDeathChecker}/\texttt{setPolicies} through the registry so they
inherit the emergency timelock on mainnet, and hold the hook owner in a multisig.
\textbf{Status: Mitigated} (bounded + fallback by construction; multisig is the
mainnet operational step).

% ---- F-15 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-15 --- \texttt{LaunchSniper} \& finalizer gate centralization \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}

\begin{finding}{sevLow}{F-15 · MiFrensGenesis / LaunchSniper · Centralization}
\textbf{Location:} \texttt{MiFrensGenesis.finalizer}, \texttt{finalize};
\texttt{LaunchSniper}.
\end{finding}

\textbf{Description.} Genesis \texttt{finalize()} (which summons iteration~1 and
seeds the pool) was permissionless. A bot could ignite first, breaking the team's
\emph{atomic} launch-plus-buy and forcing a separate, MEV-exposed buy. A
\texttt{finalizer} gate was added: if set, only that address (the
\texttt{LaunchSniper}) may finalize. \texttt{LaunchSniper.launch()} then performs
finalize $+$ tax-exempt buy $+$ forward-to-airdrop in one transaction.

\textbf{Assessment.} This is an intentional, one-time, \emph{launch}-only
centralization that improves user outcomes (no front-run, no dilution). It gates
\emph{only} genesis ignition; all subsequent relaunches remain permissionless, and
the finalizer cannot alter fund flows --- it can only be the one to press the
already-fixed ``ignite'' button. On mainnet the launch tx should be submitted via
a private mempool (Flashbots Protect) for the last mile. \textbf{Status: By
design.}

\section{Revision~3 Findings — Reserve-LP Migration Model}

\noindent The un-migrated and genesis supply is no longer held as a registry
ERC-20 \emph{balance} (which reads as a whale bag on explorers). Instead, at each
summon/relaunch the full fixed supply is placed into \textbf{two} Uniswap~v4
positions, both owned by the registry:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Active} (full range): sets the launch price + tradeable depth. Gen-1
seeds it at $10\%$; every relaunch instead \emph{recycles} it to the tokens still
in the dead pool's LP (the supply that never circulated), so the split
self-balances --- the reserve auto-sizes to exactly the prior circulating supply
(the real $1{:}1$ migration demand), less unclaimed genesis.
\item \textbf{Reserve} (single-sided token, \emph{out of range below} the launch
tick): the other $\sim\!90\%$. Since ETH is \texttt{currency0}, pool price is
\emph{tokens-per-ETH}; a token that appreciates moves the price \emph{down}, so
the reserve sits below launch and is pure \texttt{token1} until the token
appreciates $\approx\!69\times$ into the range (\texttt{RESERVE\_CEILING\_OFFSET
=42400} ticks $\approx \ln 69 / \ln 1.0001$).
\end{itemize}
Migration (\texttt{claimByBurn}, \texttt{autoMigrateBatch}) and the genesis bonus
(\texttt{claimGenesis}) release tokens $1{:}1$ from the current generation's
reserve via \texttt{decreaseLiquidity} + \texttt{TAKE\_PAIR} to the claimant ---
pure token, \emph{zero ETH, no price movement}. All V4 encoding lives in the
external linked library \texttt{PoolOps} (\texttt{delegatecall}'d, stateless),
which also let the registry runtime shrink from $25{,}117$ to $20{,}062$ bytes.
\emph{Verified live on Sepolia}: after summon the registry's loose token balance
was $3.7\times10^{-15}$ of the $777$M supply (the entire supply is in the two
positions), and a live \texttt{claimGenesis} released exactly $69{,}936.99$ tokens
($77.7\text{M}/1111$) from the reserve, decrementing its liquidity while leaving
it open.

% ---- F-16 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-16 --- Reserve exhaustion could under-fill a late migration \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}
\texttt{claimFromReserve} clamps the removed liquidity to what the position holds,
so if the reserve were ever short it would return \emph{less} than the requested
$1{:}1$ amount after the caller's old tokens are already burned. In practice the
reserve is $\sim\!90\%$ of a freshly-minted full supply each generation while the
claimable amount (circulating prior-gen supply + outstanding genesis) is bounded
well below that, so exhaustion is not reachable under normal operation.
\textbf{Recommendation:} add an explicit \texttt{require} that the post-removal
token-out $\geq$ requested (revert rather than under-fill), so a burn can never
settle for less than $1{:}1$. \textbf{Status: Acknowledged.}

% ---- F-17 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-17 --- A $\geq$69$\times$ pump sells the reserve wall for ETH \sev{sevLow}{(Low)}}
If the token trades up $\approx\!69\times$, spot enters the reserve range and the
pool begins converting reserve \texttt{token1} into ETH --- depleting the token
available for $1{:}1$ migration at the top. This is the deliberate ceiling: the
reserve doubles as deep sell-side liquidity only at valuations where a holder is
strictly better off, and the captured ETH accrues to the protocol-owned position
(recycled at relaunch). Migrators arriving post-pump receive ETH-value rather than
$1{:}1$ token. \textbf{Status: By design} (the $69\times$ ceiling was chosen
explicitly; raising \texttt{RESERVE\_CEILING\_OFFSET} hardens the peg further at
the cost of moon-time ETH capture).

% ---- F-18 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-18 --- \texttt{PoolOps} external-library delegatecall surface \sev{sevInfo}{(Info)}}
\texttt{PoolOps} is a \texttt{delegatecall}'d linked library: it executes in the
registry's context (\texttt{this}=registry, which owns the tokens/positions and is
the \texttt{msg.sender} seen by the PositionManager/Permit2). It is
\textbf{stateless}, contains no \texttt{selfdestruct} or \texttt{delegatecall} of
its own, and only calls the trusted PoolManager, PositionManager, Permit2, and the
in-house token. The link address is fixed at deploy time by the toolchain. All
reserve/claim calls occur inside \texttt{nonReentrant} registry entrypoints, so a
PositionManager callback cannot re-enter. \textbf{Status: Reviewed --- no issue.}

% ---- F-19 ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F-19 --- Claims are non-sandwichable \sev{sevInfo}{(Positive)}}
Because the reserve is \emph{out of range}, \texttt{decreaseLiquidity} returns a
deterministic token amount and moves spot price by zero. A migration/genesis claim
therefore has no MEV surface --- there is no price impact to sandwich and
\texttt{amount1Min} slippage is moot. This is a strict improvement over a
held-pool \texttt{transfer} model (which was also MEV-free but exposed the supply
as a wallet balance). \textbf{Status: Positive property.}

\section{Positive Security Properties}

The following invariants were examined and found \textbf{sound}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Non-inflationary migration.} \texttt{claimByBurn} burns the
        caller's previous-generation balance \emph{before} minting the current
        token $1{:}1$, so cross-iteration supply is conserved and no token can be
        claimed twice (dead tokens are frozen against transfer).
  \item \textbf{Airdrop follows the NFT.} \texttt{claimGenesis} keys on
        \texttt{genesisClaimed[tokenId]} and gates on live \texttt{ownerOf}, so
        the claim right transfers with secondary sales and can never double-pay.
  \item \textbf{Vault solvency.} \texttt{CauldronVault.redeem} follows CEI under
        a reentrancy guard; \texttt{floor = balance / outstanding} is preserved
        across redemptions (both numerator and denominator drop by one share).
  \item \textbf{Dividend accounting.} \texttt{MiFrensDividend} uses an $O(1)$
        MasterChef accumulator with sub-share \texttt{residual} carry; the
        presale's \texttt{finalize()} guarantees the genesis tranche is fully
        minted before any fee accrues, so no share is unclaimable.
  \item \textbf{Snapshot voting.} \texttt{CauldronGovernor} weights votes by
        \texttt{getPastVotes} at the proposal snapshot, defeating
        transfer-and-revote; one vote per address per proposal.
  \item \textbf{Reentrancy.} All external state-changing entrypoints
        (\texttt{summon}, \texttt{relaunch}, \texttt{claimByBurn},
        \texttt{claimGenesis}, vault \texttt{redeem}/\texttt{close}, dividend
        \texttt{claim}, router \texttt{play}/\texttt{playChurn}) carry
        \texttt{nonReentrant} guards and update state before external calls.
  \item \textbf{Arithmetic.} Seed price uses \texttt{FullMath.mulDiv} to avoid the
        \texttt{tokenAmount << 192} overflow; credit accrual saturates rather than
        reverting a swap.
  \item \textbf{Reduced owner surface.} Registry ownership is handed to the
        autonomous presale post-deploy, freezing owner-only configuration.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The Cauldron implements an ambitious, largely trust-minimised design: a token
lifecycle that funds and perpetuates itself from its own trading fees, with NFT
governance selecting each successor and a fee dividend rewarding the founding
guild. The core value-conserving invariants --- $1{:}1$ migration, vault
solvency, dividend accounting, and snapshot voting --- are well constructed and
defended by reentrancy guards and CEI ordering.

The two engagements identified two High, two Medium, and several lower-severity issues; the High/Medium fund-relevant ones
were remediated with minimal, well-scoped changes and are now regression-tested.
The residual risk is concentrated in the two privileged roles (F-03), which are
mitigated on-chain by an immutable emergency timelock and, for the hook, by the
new write-once registry binding. We recommend the mainnet operational hardening
in F-03 (multisig custody of both privileged roles, with a published
decommissioning path) as the final step toward the protocol's stated goal of an
``unstoppable, autonomous'' machine.
