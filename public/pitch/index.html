MiFrens03 / 13 · Insight
The core idea

Stop pretending tokens don't die.
Make death the fuel.

The Cauldron is a state machine that lives inside a Uniswap v4 pool. It launches a token, lets the market trade it, and watches the volume.

When rolling 24-hour volume falls below a threshold, the pool is dead — and because the guild has already voted on a successor, anyone can call relaunch(). In one transaction it recovers the liquidity, burns the recovered tokens, and summons the next iteration. Holders migrate 1:1.

Then it happens again. And again. Nobody has to be alive for it to work.

The token is energy — it cycles.
The fren is ownership — it never dies, and it earns from every cycle.

MiFrens05 / 13 · Architecture
Why a hook, and not a router

If you can route around it,
it isn't a rule.

Everything interesting happens inside the swap, in beforeSwap / afterSwap. That is not an aesthetic choice — it is what makes the mechanics non-bypassable.

A router-based launchpad can always be sidestepped: trade straight against the pool and you skip the fee, the volume accounting and the gacha. Because this is a hook, a raw Uniswap swap from any aggregator is charged, counted, and can forge an NFT. There is no privileged path and no way around it.

It also means leverage moves the real chart. A perp open is an actual pool swap with actual price impact — not a bet settled against an oracle.

One user swap · five contracts · one unlock
user swap
 └─ beforeSwap        charge the ETH fee on buys
 └─ PoolManager executes the swap
 └─ afterSwap
     ├─ legacy buyback      nested swap, buys the token back
     ├─ seeder poke         streams the next liquidity sliver
     ├─ liquidation sweep   real settlement swaps, in-lock
     ├─ gacha step          commits + resolves crystals, may mint
     └─ charge the fee on sells
Every side-effect is gas-bounded and result-ignored — called with a reserved budget, failure swallowed. An optional step can never revert a user's swap or run it out of gas.

Why the proposer slice is a pull

activeProposer is attacker-controlled. Pushing would put an untrusted external call in the swap hot path — so a rejecting sink never bricks a swap, and its share rolls into the relaunch reserve.

One floor, not two — the unified floor

Both collection-deployment paths wire hook.setVault(0), so there is no redeemable ETH vault. The floor share joins the buyback buffer and becomes token buy pressure too. Redeem via recycleCollectionNFT.

Percentages are of the hook fee at deployed settings (taxBps 300 · guildBps 1500 · legacyBps 4000 · floorBps 10000 · proposerBps 50), hard-capped at 10% and governance-tunable. The 84.6% arrives in two carves — legacyBps takes 33.8% off the post-guild remainder, then the 50.7% floor share follows it into the same buffer because vault == address(0). The relaunch reserve gets nothing from the split by default; it is funded by the recovered LP. Perp fees route separately: 30% genesis dividend, 70% perp stakers, side-attributed.

MiFrens08 / 13 · Floors
Not a jpeg — a perpetual claim

Three things back a fren,
and none of them is a promise.

Cash flow

The genesis dividend

Genesis MiFrens share 15% of every iteration's fees, forever. A holder "casts the spell" to switch earning on; transferring breaks the enchantment. Iteration 1 pays you, iteration 50 pays you.

Redemption

The genesis floor

Each genesis fren carries a live floor denominated in whatever token is running now, and the design ratchets it up. Burn the fren, receive its share of the reserve.

Per-collection

Collection floors

Every iteration's NFT collection earns a floor from its own volume and royalties — and keeps it forever, through every future rebirth. Recycle an NFT to claim its share.

Break-glass, stated plainly

No unilateral or instant team withdrawal exists. There is a governed emergency path that can move protocol-owned liquidity — restricted to an immutable admin (the timelock), announced, time-delayed, guardian-vetoable, and arming it forces the redemption exit open so holders can leave at the floor first.

Nobody can mint, freeze, or remove tokens or NFTs from your wallet.

The caveat we print rather than bury

The genesis floor is claimable while the token trades below its per-iteration reserve ceiling; above it, claims are bounded. That is a real limitation, documented in §7.4 of the public spec — and it is on this slide because a floor with an undisclosed bound is not a floor.

Royalties are 5% (EIP-2981 + ERC-721C): genesis to the dividend, collection royalties to that collection's own floor.

MiFrens09 / 13 · Game layer
Two systems that live inside the swap

Perps with no oracle.
NFTs forged by volume.

Perps

Leverage that moves the real chart

Every open, close and liquidation is an actual Uniswap v4 swap. Marked off a cumulative-tick TWAP the hook builds from the pool's own ticks — no external oracle, no keeper network. Any swap on any interface auto-liquidates underwater positions.

Max leveragemin(depth tier, 3×)
Open fee6.9% of collateral
Maintenance margin15%
Per-side OI cap≤30% of depth

The open fee is charged on collateral, not notional — a notional fee would start a 3× position roughly 20% underwater on day one.

Crystal gacha

Trading volume forges the other 1111

Volume banks credit — buys weighted 1.5×, sells 0.5×. Credit commits a crystal batch along a rising curve. Each ticket rolls from its commit block's hash — a value that did not exist when you played.

Max win chance90%, with a pity counter
Randomnesscommit-block hash
Router requiredno — plain swaps work
Liquidate a perpearns a Liquidatoor badge

On an L2 sequencer naive prevrandao entropy is grindable. This protocol uses none of it — and the limits of the commit-hash guarantee are documented rather than hand-waved.

Liquidating a perp engraves the kill on-chain: victim, side, leverage, entry, the mark that killed them, your bounty.

What we hardened

CREATE2 squat prevention on relaunch · gas-bounded re-entrant side-effects · deterministic O(n) force-close · exact-out sell fee bypass · reserve ceiling enforcement · governance spam and lockout resistance · storage-layout invariance across the delegatecall facet · L2 block-clock semantics.

What is still open

Known limitations are documented, not hidden — §15 of the public spec. No HIGH is open: the two that were live on round 44 are closed, and a blind red-team pass over the new code found and closed six more. That work is not yet deployed, and 44 tests are currently red on the in-flight treasury-rotation branch. Both facts are on this slide on purpose.

Fork-only tests require an archive endpoint and fail loudly rather than skipping silently — a fork test that quietly passes with no fork is worse than no test. Without the fork gate a bare run hides the entire rotation, perp and relaunch surface, and reports green.
