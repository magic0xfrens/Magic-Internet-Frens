WHAT ARE YOU BUILDING AND WHY DOES IT MATTER?
=====================================================================

We're building the Magic Internet Cauldron, an autonomous, self-funding token machine built as a Uniswap v4 hook, where the hook IS the entire economic engine rather than a fee tweak bolted onto a normal pool.

The one-line version: we are an ANTI-LAUNCHPAD. Instead of millions of random coins, one to rule them all.

Every launchpad in the market competes on emission count. The dominant launchpad on Robinhood Chain minted over 12,000 new tokens in a single 24-hour window; the launchpad it displaced voluntarily shut off new launches after clearing an estimated $12M in fees, citing low-quality tokens flooding the platform. Almost all of those pools are dead within a week, and each one leaves permanently orphaned liquidity on the chain. We think that is the actual problem in this category, and that emitting more assets cannot solve it. So we emit exactly one, and we make it survive.

One "iteration" token trades at a time in an ETH-paired v4 pool. There is no LP fee (POOL_FEE = 0); 100% of trading revenue is an ETH-denominated hook fee taken via return deltas and routed per-swap to four sinks: an NFT-holder dividend, a per-collection redemption floor, the iteration's proposer, and the reserve that funds the next launch. When a token's rolling 24-hour volume falls below a death threshold, anyone can permissionlessly trigger a relaunch: protocol-owned liquidity is recovered, a new token is deployed and seeded, and holders migrate 1:1 forever. The machine is designed to run indefinitely with no treasury top-up, because it is funded entirely by its own swap fees.

Why it matters: the launchpad category today is a sequence of disposable, extractive events. A pool is created, insiders exit, liquidity dies, the contract is abandoned, and the next one starts from zero. We're testing whether v4 hooks can make that cycle accountable instead. Value that accrues during a launch is captured on-chain in ETH, held in protocol-owned liquidity, and converted into a redemption floor that holders can actually exit into, and into the seed capital for the next launch, so each generation inherits the last one's balance sheet rather than its wreckage. Holders of retired generations never lose their claim; they migrate 1:1 or redeem at a floor that only ratchets upward.

Three things this demonstrates for v4 specifically:

1. The fee model can be currency-chosen rather than currency-imposed. Return deltas let a protocol denominate 100% of revenue in ETH on both legs of a trade, which is impossible under an LP fee because LP fees always accrue in the input currency.

2. Real leverage can live inside a swap. Our perpetuals open, close and liquidate through actual pool swaps. No external oracle, no synthetic book, real price impact, with liquidations swept opportunistically from afterSwap so any trade on any interface can trigger one and earn the keeper reward.

3. Launch fairness can be structural. Liquidity streams into the pool progressively from inside afterSwap using core modifyLiquidity. It is keeperless, and un-frontrunnable because the deployed fraction is a pure function of elapsed time. In fork tests a block-0 whale receives roughly 8x fewer tokens for roughly 23x the price impact of a later buyer. That is anti-snipe by book shape, not by punitive tax.

It matters to us that this is genuinely v4-native. None of the above is portable to v2 or v3. If it works, it is an argument that hooks change what a token launch is, not just what it costs.

ARE YOU USING A CUSTOM ACCOUNTING APPROACH? DOES YOUR HOOK USE
beforeSwapReturnDelta OR afterSwapReturnDelta?
=====================================================================

Yes, both.

The hook declares:

  afterInitialize:       true    (adopt only pools our registry created)
  beforeSwap:            true    (charge the ETH fee on buys)
  afterSwap:             true    (charge the ETH fee on sells)
  beforeSwapReturnDelta: true
  afterSwapReturnDelta:  true
  all other permissions: false   (no initialize, donate, or liquidity callbacks)

Encoded flag bits 0x10cc. The deployed address 0x31ee3436d96533e91874cda5dfc70cb521f750cc carries exactly those low bits and no others, verifiable with uint160(hook) & Hooks.ALL_HOOK_MASK. We hold no liquidity-callback or donate permissions, so the hook cannot interpose on modifyLiquidity or donate at all.

Why both flags are load-bearing. The hook's economics require the fee to be denominated in ETH on every trade. In v4, the afterSwap return delta can only act on the unspecified currency. With currency0 as native ETH, the four quadrants are:

  exact-in BUY  (zeroForOne, amt<0): specified ETH in,  unspecified token out - afterSwap could only charge in TOKEN (unusable)
  exact-out BUY (zeroForOne, amt>0): specified token out, unspecified ETH in  - afterSwap could charge in ETH (usable)
  exact-in SELL (!zeroForOne, amt<0): specified token in, unspecified ETH out - afterSwap could charge in ETH (usable)
  exact-out SELL(!zeroForOne, amt>0): specified ETH out,  unspecified token in - afterSwap could only charge in TOKEN (unusable)

The exact-input buy is the dominant trade and is what the Uniswap interface and every aggregator emit, and it has the token as its unspecified leg. Charging it in ETH therefore requires skimming the specified input in beforeSwap, which means beforeSwapReturnDelta. There is no alternative mechanism.

afterSwapReturnDelta charges the sell side. Without it the hook cannot take value from a swap at all, because a poolManager.take() with no offsetting return delta leaves an unsettled negative delta and the unlock reverts with CurrencyNotSettled.

We evaluated the delta-free alternative and rejected it on merit. A dynamic LP fee (DYNAMIC_FEE_FLAG plus a beforeSwap override) needs no delta flag, and our LP is protocol-owned so the fee would land in our own positions. We did not choose it, for three reasons:

1. LP fees accrue in the input currency (Pool.sol: zeroForOne ? feeGrowthGlobal0 : feeGrowthGlobal1), so every sell would accrue the retiring token, which is exactly what the ETH-only fee exists to prevent.

2. It would introduce a keeper. Our positions sit in the periphery PositionManager, which opens its own unlock and therefore cannot be harvested inside a swap. Per-swap routing would become a batch harvest.

3. It would be JIT-extractable. We hold no liquidity-callback permissions, so LPing into our pools is permissionless. Under an LP fee a searcher could JIT a concentrated position around a large swap and capture the fee, including the launch surtax, inverting the mechanic it exists for. Under the delta model this is structurally impossible, because the hook takes the fee directly regardless of who provides liquidity.

The delta flags make the hook safer, not less safe, and they are also precisely why we want a professional audit before mainnet routing.


=====================================================================
UNIQUE FEATURES OF YOUR HOOK AND THE COMPLEXITIES THEY INTRODUCE
(EXECUTION FLOW, ACCOUNTING LOGIC, EDGE CASES, ARCHITECTURE)
=====================================================================

CUSTOM ACCOUNTING: FOUR CONTRACTS SETTLING IN ONE UNLOCK

A single swap can involve four settling parties: the hook, the registry's liquidity library, the perp engine, and the progressive seeder. Each settles its own deltas in its own frame. Two details our internal audit specifically verified:

- The legacy buyback settles the realised debit read from the returned delta, not the intended amount, so a binding price limit cannot leave a positive un-taken delta and revert the user's swap.
- The perp engine switches to an in-place swap when it is already inside the lock, rather than re-entering unlock.

Both are exactly the class of bug an auditor should be paid to hunt, and both are why we are applying.

afterSwap DOES OPTIONAL WORK, AND NONE OF IT CAN BREAK A SWAP

afterSwap may trigger a nested buyback swap, a seeder liquidity placement, a perp-liquidation sweep, and an NFT mint. Every one is invoked with a reserved gas budget and an ignored result:

  uint256 g = gasleft();
  if (g > LIQ_GAS_RESERVE + LIQ_GAS_MIN) {
      perpEngine.call{gas: g - LIQ_GAS_RESERVE}(...);   // result ignored
  }

An optional step that runs out of gas or reverts is a silent no-op, and the parent swap always completes its fee take and returns. Reserves are held independently for the liquidation sweep, the gacha step, the buyback, and the seeder poke. Nested swaps are guarded by transient flags (_inSelfBuy, _inRelaunchClose) so they never re-enter fee logic or recurse. Gas work brought short-liquidation cost from 957k down to roughly 450k specifically so a normal-sized swap can afford to trigger one.

POOL ADOPTION IS CLOSED

v4 lets anyone initialise a pool naming any hook, so _afterInitialize is an untrusted entrypoint. We adopt a pool only if sender == registry AND currency0 is native ETH. A foreign pool naming this hook is declined rather than reverted, so we cannot be used to grief pool creation, and every value-moving path is gated on the adoption flag, meaning such a pool gets no fee logic, no accounting and no spending. This was a Critical finding in our own review, C-01, now fixed with regression tests.

FEE BOUNDS, INCLUDING THE ONE THAT DESERVES SCRUTINY

  Base hook fee:   300 bps (3%), governance-tunable, hard-capped at MAX_TAX_BPS = 1000 (10%)
  Launch surtax:   up to 9,600 bps. Time-decaying anti-snipe, linear decay to ZERO across
                   snipeWindowBlocks (default 30 blocks). Routed 100% to NFT holders.
  Absolute clamp:  9,900 bps (MAX_TOTAL_FEE_BPS). A swap always retains at least 1%,
                   so it can never return zero.
  LP fee tier:     0. Traders pay the hook fee only; there is no stacked LP fee.

We flag the surtax explicitly because a hook that can take 99% of a swap deserves a second look. It exists only in the opening minutes of a freshly summoned pool, decays deterministically to zero, is bounded by an immutable constant ceiling, and is paid to token holders rather than to us. Steady state is 3%.

DISCLOSED INTEGRATION DEFECT: EXACT-OUTPUT SELLS CURRENTLY REVERT

_beforeSwap reverts with ExactOutSellUnsupported for the (amountSpecified > 0, !zeroForOne) quadrant. The reason is the quadrant table above: the unspecified leg there is the token, so v4's return-delta mechanism physically cannot take an ETH fee. The prior behaviour served that quadrant for free, which was a measured fee bypass (0 wei versus 4.24e15 wei on the identical exact-input sell) that also accrued crystal credit and lifetime volume. We now refuse it rather than serve it free.

We accept that reverting a whole quadrant is a poor router citizen, and it is the one item we consider a genuine integration defect rather than a trade-off. Because the specified currency in that quadrant IS ETH, the fee can be taken via beforeSwapReturnDelta on the specified side, which we already hold, so the fix requires no additional permission and no address re-mine. It is scheduled into the pre-audit freeze. Every other quadrant is served today.

NOT UPGRADEABLE, AND THE CUSTODY POWER WE ARE DISCLOSING ANYWAY

The hook is a plain contract. No proxy, no delegatecall in the hook, no admin that can replace its code. Three things could superficially read as upgradeability:

1. Policy modules (IDeathChecker, ISurtaxPolicy, IOddsPolicy, ICurvePolicy, IFeeRouter). Each is a view that returns a number. It never custodies or moves funds, its output is clamped to the same immutable ceilings as the built-in rule, and a reverting module falls back to the built-in rule. IFeeRouter returns amounts only; the hook performs the sends, and a split that does not sum exactly to the fee is discarded in favour of the built-in split.

2. Controller pointer. setRegistry is one-shot. Replacing it requires proposeRegistryOverride, then a REGISTRY_SWAP_DELAY of 7 days declared as a constant so the owner cannot shorten their own notice period, then executeRegistryOverride, which flushes the accrued fee reserve to the outgoing controller first so a swap cannot capture ETH the previous controller accrued.

3. Ownership. The hook is owned by an OpenZeppelin TimelockController, an audited standard chosen specifically to avoid an upgradeable proxy on the money path. Every parameter change is schedule, wait, execute.

Break-glass, stated in full. emergencyWithdrawLP and emergencySweep on the registry can remove a generation's protocol-owned liquidity. Four things bound it: the emergency admin is immutable, set in the constructor with no setter, and it is the governance timelock; actions are announced on-chain and delayed; an independent guardian can veto during that window and can only ever cancel, never propose or move funds; and arming forces the redemption exit open, so it cannot be used to trap holders ahead of a custody move. Governance cannot mint tokens, freeze balances, pause transfers, touch any token or NFT held in a user's wallet, or shorten its own delays. None of this is reachable from the hook. These are registry functions over protocol-owned liquidity and cannot affect swap execution, settlement, or any routed trade.

hookData IS OPTIONAL ON EVERY PATH

An empty hookData is a designed-for case, not a degraded one. Fee tier falls back to sender. NFT credit falls back to tx.origin, which is deliberate and documented, because crediting sender would send the NFT to the router instead of the buyer; ERC-4337 users should route through a tagging opener, and creditUntaggedSwaps can disable the path entirely. Fee exemption requires isOpener[sender], an explicit allowlist, so an arbitrary caller cannot tag an exempt address and dodge the fee.

KNOWN ARCHITECTURAL CONSTRAINT

The hook sits 88 bytes below the EIP-170 limit. We have already done one facet split, a shared-storage base plus a delegatecall RedemptionExt facet with byte-identical storage layouts verified mechanically across 53 entries, to reclaim 3.4KB. This is a process constraint on the audit: scope should be frozen before review, because we cannot absorb large remediation diffs into the hook without another extraction. We would rather the auditor know this on day one.

PLEASE EXPLAIN THE TECH OF YOUR UNISWAP PRODUCT / INTEGRATION,
FIRST IN ELI5, THEN IN TECHNICAL DEPTH
=====================================================================

ELI5

Imagine a cauldron that brews one magic coin at a time.

Every time someone buys or sells that coin, the cauldron takes a small pinch of ETH, never the coin itself, because the coin is temporary and the ETH is not. That ETH goes four places: to the people holding the original NFTs, forever; into a floor fund that guarantees you can always sell your coin back for a real price; to whoever proposed the current brew; and into a savings pot for the next brew.

When a coin stops being traded, and the cauldron watches this itself, on-chain, anybody can ring the bell. The old coin's liquidity is pulled back out, a brand-new coin is brewed with it, and everyone who held the old coin swaps 1:1 into the new one. Nobody gets left holding a dead bag. That is the whole point.

Along the way, buying the coin randomly forges collectible creatures, like a gacha machine built into the trade itself. And you can take leveraged bets on the coin using the same pool everyone else trades in, so your bet actually moves the price, and when someone gets liquidated it happens inside somebody else's ordinary trade.

The clever part is that all of this lives inside Uniswap itself, in a piece of code called a hook, instead of in a separate app bolted on the side.

TECHNICAL DEPTH

Architecture. Around 30 Solidity files, roughly 10,200 raw lines and 5,400 nSLOC:

  CauldronHook.sol (1,863 lines) - the v4 hook. Fee engine, volume and death oracle,
      crystal accrual, gacha trigger, liquidation sweep, buyback, seeder poke.
      Permissions 0x10cc.
  CauldronRegistry.sol (1,468) plus CauldronBase.sol and RedemptionExt.sol - the
      orchestrator. summon() genesis, permissionless relaunch(), 1:1 claimTokens(gen),
      redemption, protocol-owned-liquidity management. Facet-split for EIP-170 with a
      mechanically verified identical storage layout.
  PerpEngine.sol (1,404) plus PerpVault.sol and PerpStakerOracle.sol - hook-native
      longs and shorts.
  PoolOps.sol (821) - v4 liquidity operations, token deployment, Permit2 plumbing.
  CauldronSeeder.sol (484) plus SeedLib.sol - progressive in-swap liquidity streaming.
  MiFrensGenesis.sol (612), CauldronCollection.sol, MiFrensDividend.sol,
      CauldronGachaRouter.sol - the NFT and dividend layer. ERC-721C, EIP-2981
      royalties routed to the genesis dividend.
  CauldronGovernor.sol, MigrationVesting.sol, CollectionLedger.sol, ReserveLib.sol,
      DefaultFeeRouter.sol, CauldronToken.sol (fixed-supply, non-mintable,
      non-freezable).

Swap execution flow:

  beforeSwap
    - self-buy or relaunch-close flag set?     -> zero delta, no fee
    - pool not adopted (_served)?              -> zero delta, no fee
    - exact-out SELL quadrant?                 -> revert ExactOutSellUnsupported
    - not an exact-input ETH-in buy?           -> zero delta (afterSwap handles it)
    - fee-exempt opener?                       -> zero delta
    - otherwise skim the ETH fee off the specified input
        -> toBeforeSwapDelta(int128(fee), 0), so only (amountIn - fee) is swapped

  afterSwap
    - self-buy or relaunch-close?              -> no-op
    - pool not adopted?                        -> no-op
    - maybe legacy buyback        (gas-reserved, result ignored)
    - maybe seeder poke           (gas-reserved, result ignored)
    - volume accounting -> 24h rolling window -> isDead() oracle
    - crystal credit -> buyer (hookData tag, else tx.origin), buy/sell weighted
    - maybe perp liquidation sweep (gas-reserved, result ignored)
    - maybe gacha forge, commit-reveal (gas-reserved)
    - on SELLs, take the ETH fee via afterSwapReturnDelta

  fee routing, per swap, ETH-denominated
    - proposerBps -> proposerOwed[proposer]  (PULL, never push - reentrancy fix A-01)
    - guildBps    -> genesis MiFrens dividend
    - floorBps    -> the active brew's floor vault
    - remainder   -> relaunchETH reserve, which funds the next generation

Perpetuals. Opens, closes and liquidations are real pool swaps against the same pool as spot, so leverage carries genuine price impact and there is no basis between a perp price and the market. Liquidation marks come from the engine's own on-chain TWAP ring, OBS_CARDINALITY = 32. Underwater positions are swept opportunistically from afterSwap under a gas reserve, so any swap on any interface can trigger a liquidation and earn the keeper reward, keeperBps = 145, roughly 0.1% of collateral. MAX_OPEN_POSITIONS = 64 bounds force-close work. A frontend guard prevents opening a position that would be born underwater against the TWAP.

Progressive seeding. Launch liquidity streams into the pool over a configurable window via core modifyLiquidity, called from afterSwap. It is keeperless, with a permissionless poke() fallback and a governance kill-switch, hook.setSeeder(0). The deployed fraction is a pure function of elapsed time, so it cannot be frontrun. Fork-measured: a block-0 whale gets roughly 8x fewer tokens for roughly 23x the price impact of a later buyer.

The floor. A recycle-ratchet. Recycling a creature routes ETH to the treasury, which buys back into the reserve, growing it, so the floor only rises. The reserve is a single-sided out-of-range band, and a CollectionLedger provides O(1) accounting so every collection, not just genesis, has a self-funded floor.

Migration. 1:1 or it reverts. MigrationVesting drips old-to-new-generation claims linearly, governance-settable and bounded between 1 hour and 14 days, with perp stakers exempt, enforced by a registry claim gate.

HOW WILL YOUR PROJECT BENEFIT THE UNISWAP ECOSYSTEM?
=====================================================================

1. It routes 100% of a token's lifetime volume through v4, permanently. There is no bonding curve, no graduation, no separate venue. Spot trading, leverage, liquidations, launch seeding, buybacks, redemption and rebirth are all v4 swaps and modifyLiquidity calls against the PoolManager. Every mechanic we add is v4 volume.

2. It is a worked reference implementation of return-delta accounting. We wrote a public derivation of why the exact-input-buy quadrant forces beforeSwapReturnDelta if you want ETH-denominated fees, why the dynamic-LP-fee alternative is JIT-extractable when you hold no liquidity permissions, and why exact-output sells are structurally unservable in an ETH-fee-only design. We found a measured fee bypass in our own hook from exactly this asymmetry, finding Z-01. That table and that bug class generalise to every hook that wants single-currency fees. It is a mistake other teams will make, and we have written it up publicly so they do not have to.

3. Adversarial findings we are publishing back. Our internal red-team produced findings we believe are ecosystem-relevant beyond us: a CREATE2 token-squat on deterministic per-generation token deployment, Critical, fixed by moving to plain CREATE; stale-lastTick TWAP poisoning in in-hook oracles, High; the _afterInitialize untrusted-adoption pattern, Critical, since anyone can initialise a pool naming your hook; and delta-settlement failures when multiple contracts settle inside one unlock. Exploit proofs-of-concept live in test/attacks/ with written reports. Whatever a professional audit adds, we will publish too.

4. Aggregator-friendly hook behaviour as a norm. We treat the router-less, empty-hookData swap as the primary case, not the fallback. That is the behaviour the ecosystem needs hooks to have if v4 liquidity is going to be routable by default, and we are trying to demonstrate it is achievable without giving up hook-native mechanics.

5. Composability we cannot reach without an audit. Being routable through the Uniswap interface, being safely aggregated, and being integrated by third parties are all downstream of a professional review. The subsidy converts a self-audited testnet system into ecosystem-usable liquidity.

6. A new fee-recycling primitive. ETH-denominated, protocol-owned, recycled into protocol-owned liquidity and an enforced floor. If it works, it is an alternative to the extract-and-abandon launch pattern that currently produces most of the dead pools on every chain.


=====================================================================
DOES YOUR PROJECT INTERACT WITH OTHER DEFI PROTOCOLS, AND IF SO, HOW?
=====================================================================

Deliberately, our external surface is narrow. The fewer external dependencies on the money path, the smaller the audit and the fewer the trust assumptions.

Uniswap v4, our core dependency:
- PoolManager: initialize, swap, modifyLiquidity, take and settle. The seeder and perp engine call core modifyLiquidity and swap directly from inside a lock; the registry uses periphery for protocol-owned liquidity.
- PositionManager (v4-periphery): protocol-owned liquidity positions. A V2 handoff is designed as a position-NFT ownership transfer rather than a teardown, so price and liquidity are untouched across an upgrade.
- Permit2: required by periphery's _pay, which pulls ERC-20s via Permit2 rather than a plain approve. The registry approves Permit2 and grants the PositionManager an allowance before modifyLiquidities. Without this, summon() and relaunch() revert on real v4.

OpenZeppelin:
- TimelockController owns the hook and the perp engine, and is the registry's immutable emergency admin. Chosen specifically to avoid an upgradeable proxy on the money path.

The floor component is monotonic by construction, because the recycle-ratchet means it can only increase.
